Word detail: four full-page layouts

Tapping a card opens the word as a full page, so every layout here uses the whole screen, with no cards inside it. Each one keeps the simplified card's format: the word at 52 pt and one idea at a time in 32 pt bold. Each phone is live: swipe, tap, or use the arrow keys.

  • Dragging down still closes the page, so moving between chunks is a short sideways swipe or a tap. B is the only one that scrolls vertically, and there dragging down closes the page only from the first chunk.
  • Each layout has a signifier: story segments, the next chunk peeking at the bottom, a labelled button, or the edges of a pile.

A · Stories recommended

Full pages that slide sideways, with story segments at the top.

Signifier
Six segments at the top, which everyone reads as "there are more of these". On the first visit, "Tap to continue" sits at the bottom until you move on.
Gesture
Tap the right of the screen for the next page and the left for the previous one. A short sideways swipe also works and follows your finger.
Motion
The page slides 1:1 while its text blurs and fades. The segment fills as you swipe. Once the big word leaves, the small "lambent" takes its place at the top.
References
Instagram stories, Life Reset ("Tap anywhere to continue"), Oura year in review

B · Scroll

The word stays pinned. Chunks scroll up beneath it, and the next one waits at the bottom.

Signifier
The next chunk's label and the start of its text sit at the bottom of the page, faded, with a small up chevron.
Gesture
A short swipe up snaps to the next chunk. Tapping the peek does the same. On the first chunk, dragging down closes the page.
Motion
The peeking chunk rises into place and sharpens. The current one rises under the word and dissolves.
References
Deepstash (next card waits at the bottom), Google Photos memories, Strava "Swipe up for details"

C · Next

The word stays put. A big labelled button names what comes next.

Signifier
The button names the next chunk ("In a sentence →"), and progress segments sit above it.
Gesture
Tap. A short sideways swipe does the same.
Motion
The text is swapped with a blur replace: the old text rises out of focus and the new text rises into focus. The button's label blurs into the next name.
References
The Outsiders word page, Moonlitt

E · Pile

Each chunk is a full page in a pile. Throw the top page away, like on the deck, to see the one under it.

Signifier
The bottom edges of the pages underneath show below the page, with "1 of 6" on it.
Gesture
A short swipe either way throws the page off: left goes forward, right goes back. Tapping goes forward.
Motion
The page stays crisp under your finger and blurs once it is thrown. The page beneath rises from its edge into focus.
Watch out
It is the deck's own gesture, so people may expect it to move to the next word.

Recommendation: A · Stories

Story segments are the most widely understood way to say "there's more, tap through". Tapping takes no drag at all, so it never competes with the drag down that closes the page. Every page is still a full, simplified card. B is the runner-up if you want reading to feel continuous rather than paged.

Questions

  • Chunk order and names. Meaning → in a sentence → also means → similar words → origin → remember it?
  • Text that's too long. At 32 pt a page holds about 9 lines. A long definition could step down to 26 pt (like CardFace's minimumScaleFactor), or split across two pages.
