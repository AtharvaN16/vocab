Ask anything: three ways in

Today you reach Ask AI by swiping a word's card up. The card parks at the top and every question is about that word, grounded on its Wiktionary facts. These three shapes let you ask without a word. They are low-fi but use the app's own pieces: the grey card on the page, the round 64 pt buttons, the frameless centred composer and the model pill. Orange outlines mark what is new.

Your own probe results apply here. Ungrounded, a small model inverts the meaning of rare words. So any general answer that names words should look each one up in Wiktionary before showing it, and take its gloss from there. Concept C does this.

C · SEARCH OR ASK

One field: type a word and it searches, type a question and it asks

When you don't have a word in mind you already go to Search, so the general Ask lives there. A single word works as today. Once you type a phrase or a question, the top row becomes Ask. The most useful general question in a vocab app is the reverse dictionary, “a word for …”. Its answer is a list of real words: each one is looked up in Wiktionary, its gloss comes from there, and each has Add.

If I had to pick

C, plus A's chip. C is where you already go without a word in mind, and its answer shape (real words, checked, with Add) is exactly the app's job. A's ✕ chip covers the other road in: you are on a word and the question drifts wider. Both reuse AskView's streaming and choreography. Only the prompt changes: no DICTIONARY FACTS block, plus a structured “candidate words” output that the app checks against Wiktionary.

B is worth it only if AI should be the headline of the app rather than a tool inside it.
